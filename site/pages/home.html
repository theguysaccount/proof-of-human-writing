How it works

  1. Write here. The editor records each change to your text and when it happened. Your draft saves itself in your browser, so you can come back to it tomorrow.
  2. Export the replay. One small file holds your text and the recording. Short pieces also fit in a link.
  3. Your reader opens it. On the reader page they see the finished text, a chart of how it grew, anything that was pasted, and a replay they can play at any speed.

What a replay shows, and what it can't

A replay is a recording, not a certificate. The reader page says this to everyone who opens one.

  • It shows how a text was entered into this editor: typed key by key, revised, pasted, and when.
  • It can't show who was at the keyboard, or what was on another screen. Text can be retyped from somewhere else.
  • It can be faked. An auto-typing program can imitate a person, and someone with the skill can fabricate a replay file.

What it gives an honest writer is what a suspicious reader usually asks for: the drafts, the hesitations and the second thoughts, in order. More on limits and privacy

Private by design

  • No upload. The editor and the reader run in your browser. Each page carries a security policy that blocks it from sending data anywhere; you can check it yourself.
  • No account, no cookies. Your draft lives in this browser's storage until you delete it.
  • Less than a keylogger. Timings are rounded to a twentieth of a second, and how long you hold each key is never recorded.
  • You choose who sees it. A replay contains your text and your typing rhythm. Send it to people you'd show a draft to.
