# Product: Writing Replay

**User.** Students and writers who need to show that a piece is their own work; teachers and editors who ask them to.

**Painful job.** "Prove you didn't use AI." AI detectors judge only the finished text and misfire, especially on non-native English writers. Writers have no simple, private way to show *how* they wrote something.

**Promise.** Write here; get a replay anyone can watch: typing, pauses, corrections, pastes. Nothing leaves your device unless you send it.

**Wedge.** The reader is free and needs nothing installed, so every replay a writer sends puts the tool in front of a teacher or editor, who can then ask the whole class for replays. Search: "prove I didn't use AI", "flagged by AI detector", "writing replay", "Google Docs version history teacher".

**Why now.** Detectors are being withdrawn or switched off (OpenAI, Vanderbilt), but the suspicion remains. Process evidence is what institutions are moving towards.

**Activation metric.** A writer exports a replay (file or link). Share loop: a reader opens one.

**First revenue event.** A school or publication pays for hosted replays and class collection (needs other hosting; `SCHOOLS_URL` slot).

**v1 deliberately won't:** record video or screens; record key-hold timings (biometric); store anything server-side; issue "verified human" badges or scores; claim to prove authorship.
